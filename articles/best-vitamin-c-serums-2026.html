Best Vitamin C Serums in 2026: A Side-by-Side Comparison

Vitamin C serums vary widely in form, strength and feel. We compare five well-known options using the brands' own product information, then explain how to choose the right one for your skin.

Updated November 2026 · 12 min read

How We Compared These Serums

This comparison is based on the brands' published product information, such as ingredient lists, stated concentrations and usage directions. It is not based on our own hands-on testing, and we do not claim visible results for any product. Formulas, sizes and prices change and can differ by country, so check the label and current listing of the version you buy.

We chose five widely available serums that represent different approaches: a pure L-ascorbic acid formula with antioxidants, a booster you can mix into your routine, a high-strength budget option, a blend of vitamin C forms and a gentler derivative. Each one is described only with details the brand or retailer lists.

Why Vitamin C Is in So Many Routines

Vitamin C is an antioxidant that is widely used in skincare to support a brighter-looking, more even tone, and it is usually applied in the morning under sunscreen. The challenge is stability. Pure L-ascorbic acid is the most widely studied form, but it can be unstable and, at higher strengths, irritating for some skin types. Derivatives such as ascorbyl glucoside are generally positioned as gentler options. Neither form replaces sunscreen.

1. SkinCeuticals C E Ferulic

The listing we checked states 15% L-ascorbic acid, 1% alpha tocopherol (vitamin E) and 0.5% ferulic acid. It is a classic combination formula: the vitamin E and ferulic acid are included to work alongside the vitamin C as part of an antioxidant system.

The directions say to apply 4 to 5 drops to a dry face, neck and chest in the morning, after cleansing and toning and before other anti-aging products. A one-ounce bottle is listed.

May suit: people who want a well-known, straightforward antioxidant serum and are comfortable with L-ascorbic acid.
Watch out for: a premium positioning compared with most options here, so check the current price. Pure L-ascorbic acid can sting on sensitive skin.

2. Paula's Choice C15 Super Booster

The brand's page lists 15% stabilized vitamin C as L-ascorbic acid at a pH of 3.1 to 3.5, with ferulic acid, ergothioneine, vitamin E and peptides among the other ingredients. It is sold as a booster: you can apply a few drops on their own or mix them into other serums or a moisturizer that does not contain sunscreen.

The page recommends once or twice daily, says less frequent use still offers benefits, and advises against mixing it into sunscreen or using it together with copper peptides at the same time. The packaging is described as UV-coated glass with an internal restrictor to limit air exposure.

May suit: people who like to customize their routine or want to reduce the amount applied if their skin is sensitive.
Watch out for: the brand's own guidance to reduce frequency if you notice dryness, redness or stinging. Formulations can vary by region, so check the label.

3. The Ordinary Vitamin C Suspension 23% + HA Spheres 2%

The brand describes this as 23% pure L-ascorbic acid in a suspension with 2% hyaluronic acid spheres. The directions say to apply a small amount once daily, ideally in the evening, to the face while avoiding the eye contour, and to use sun protection during the day.

The page notes that some tingling is expected, that the texture is slightly granular, and that you can mix it with your preferred oil if the tingling is too strong. It advises a patch test and lists products it should not be used with, including direct acids, retinoids, niacinamide and copper peptides.

May suit: experienced vitamin C users who want a high-strength, low-cost formula and do not mind a different texture.
Watch out for: the high concentration. Beginners and sensitive skin may find a lower-strength or derivative formula more comfortable.

4. Naturium Vitamin C Complex Serum

The retail listing describes a blend of L-ascorbic acid and sodium ascorbyl phosphate, which the listing says is pure vitamin C conjugated to gold particles, with glycerin as a humectant and a fruit complex from mango, papaya and pineapple. The listing does not state the percentage of any vitamin C form.

The directions say to shake well and apply an even layer to the face, neck and chest morning and night, avoiding the eye area, then follow with moisturizer and use SPF in the morning. A patch test is advised.

May suit: people who want a serum that mixes more than one vitamin C form and is designed for twice-daily use.
Watch out for: the missing concentration on the listing we checked, which makes direct strength comparisons difficult.

5. The Ordinary Ascorbyl Glucoside Solution 12%

This is a water-based serum built around 12% ascorbyl glucoside, a water-soluble vitamin C derivative. The brand's page lists it for vitamin C beginners, sensitive skin, oily or combination skin and everyday use, and says it is suitable for all skin types.

The directions say to apply a few drops morning and evening after cleansing and before heavier serums, oils or creams, and to use a high-SPF sunscreen in the morning. It does not need refrigeration but should be kept away from direct sunlight.

May suit: first-time users and anyone who has found pure L-ascorbic acid too strong.
Watch out for: derivatives are generally positioned as gentler, so the effect may be more gradual. Individual results vary.

How to Choose a Vitamin C Serum

Start with the form

L-ascorbic acid is the form most associated with strong results and also with the most irritation and instability. Derivatives such as ascorbyl glucoside or sodium ascorbyl phosphate are generally considered gentler. If you are new to vitamin C or have sensitive skin, a derivative is a reasonable place to start.

Consider the concentration

A higher percentage is not automatically better. Higher strengths can be harder to tolerate, and the pH and formula matter as much as the number. Begin with a lower strength or less frequent use and increase only if your skin is comfortable.

Look at the packaging

Vitamin C degrades with light and air. Opaque or UV-coated bottles with a dropper or pump that limits air exposure help the serum last longer.

Match it to your skin and routine

Oily or resilient skin often tolerates L-ascorbic acid well. Dry or reactive skin may prefer a derivative or a booster you can mix into moisturizer. If you already use retinoids or exfoliating acids, introduce vitamin C slowly and follow each brand's guidance on what not to combine.

How to Use Vitamin C Serum

Most routines apply vitamin C in the morning after cleansing and before moisturizer, followed by sunscreen. Some products are designed for evening or twice-daily use, so follow the directions on your bottle. Patch-test on a small area first, avoid the eye area unless the brand says otherwise, and use fewer applications per week if you notice stinging, redness or dryness. If irritation continues, stop using the product and speak with a dermatologist or pharmacist.

How to Tell When a Serum Has Oxidized

A serum that has darkened to orange or brown, or that smells noticeably different, has likely oxidized and may no longer perform as intended. Store it away from light and heat, keep the cap closed tightly and replace it when it changes noticeably.

Our Takeaway

There is no single best vitamin C serum for everyone. A gentler derivative suits beginners and sensitive skin, a classic L-ascorbic acid formula with ferulic acid and vitamin E suits those who want a traditional antioxidant serum, and a high-strength budget option suits experienced users. Choose the form and strength your skin can tolerate, use it consistently and always pair it with sunscreen.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does vitamin C serum take to work?

Results depend on the product, your skin and consistency. Many people use a serum for several weeks before judging its effect on tone and brightness, and individual results vary.

Can I use vitamin C with retinol?

Many routines use vitamin C in the morning and retinol in the evening to avoid layering strong actives together. Follow the guidance on each product, and introduce one new active at a time.

Why did my vitamin C serum turn orange or brown?

This usually indicates oxidation, which happens when vitamin C is exposed to light, air and heat. A noticeably darkened serum may be less effective, so consider replacing it.

Is vitamin C safe for sensitive skin?

Some people with sensitive skin tolerate gentler derivatives better than pure L-ascorbic acid. Patch-test first, start slowly and stop use if irritation develops. If you have a skin condition, ask a dermatologist.

Do I still need sunscreen with vitamin C?

Yes. Vitamin C is an antioxidant, not a sunscreen. Apply a broad-spectrum sunscreen every morning.
